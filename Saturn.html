Saturn
Cassini's final dive

Introduction

Iconic rings

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second largest in the solar system, best known for its spectacular system of rings. Like Jupiter, it is a gas giant made mostly of hydrogen and helium, with no solid surface. It is so light for its size that it is the only planet less dense than water, and it has more than 270 known moons.

Research Data

Missions to Saturn

  • 1979: NASA's Pioneer 11 makes the first flyby of Saturn, passing close to the cloud tops and the rings.
  • 1980–1981: Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 fly past, sending back detailed images of the rings and moons, including Titan's thick atmosphere.
  • 1997: The Cassini–Huygens mission, a joint project of NASA, ESA and the Italian space agency, launches.
  • 2004: Cassini arrives and becomes the first spacecraft to orbit Saturn.
  • 2005: The Huygens probe lands on Titan, the first landing on a moon in the outer solar system.
  • 2017: Cassini ends its mission with a series of dives between Saturn and its rings, then plunges into the planet's atmosphere on purpose to avoid contaminating moons that could host life.
  • Planned: NASA's Dragonfly, a car-sized helicopter-like rotorcraft, is being built to explore Titan, with launch planned for 2028 and arrival in the mid-2030s.
