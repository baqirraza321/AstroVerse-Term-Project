Introduction

Supersonic winds

Neptune is the eighth and farthest planet from the Sun, a dark, cold, and windy ice giant about four times the width of Earth. It is made mostly of water, ammonia, and methane over a small rocky core, and the methane gives it its deep blue colour. Too faint to see with the naked eye, it was the first planet found by mathematics before it was ever spotted through a telescope.

Research Data

Missions to Neptune

  • 1846: Neptune is found after astronomers predict its position from irregularities in Uranus's orbit. Johann Galle sees it through a telescope on the night he receives the prediction. Its moon Triton is discovered just weeks later.
  • 1989: NASA's Voyager 2 makes the only flyby ever, discovering the Great Dark Spot, Triton's geysers, and six new moons.
  • 1994: The Hubble Space Telescope finds that the Great Dark Spot has vanished, and new dark spots later appear. Storms on Neptune come and go.
  • 2011: Neptune completes its first full orbit since its discovery.
  • 2022: The James Webb Space Telescope captures its sharpest infrared images of Neptune's rings.
